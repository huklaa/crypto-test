CHAINLING BLOG

Robinhood Chain, Base and Arbitrum Orbit

1. Early Stage Opportunities on Robinhood Chain and the Future of L2s

Robinhood Chain is one of the newest representatives of Layer-2 (L2) solutions aiming to relieve the transaction load on Ethereum. Early-stage opportunities generally center around the following points:

First Mover Advantage: Since the network is very new, competition is low. The first NFT marketplaces, the first DEXs (Decentralized Exchanges), and the first bridge protocols gather the core liquidity of the network. For investors, getting involved in these projects early means benefiting from potential future airdrops or high staking returns.

Real World Assets (RWA) Focus: Robinhood Chain has a narrative aimed at bridging the gap between traditional finance and DeFi (Decentralized Finance). This signals that not only speculative tokens but also tokenized real-world assets will be traded on the network.

The Future of L2s: L2s are no longer just “cheaper Ethereum”; they are building their own ecosystems and cultural structures. Communities that adopt this culture at an early stage have the power to shape the direction of the network.

2. Differences from a Developer's Perspective: Base Network vs. Robinhood Chain Comparison

These two popular L2 networks have distinct differences in terms of the technologies behind them and the developer experience:

Infrastructure (OP Stack vs. Arbitrum Orbit): Base is built on Optimism's OP Stack architecture. This allows Base to be a part of the Superchain vision and easily integrate with other OP-based networks. Robinhood Chain, on the other hand, uses Arbitrum Orbit. Orbit provides developers with much more customization, from block times to the Data Availability layer.

Development and Integration Experience: The architecture encountered when building a portfolio analytics demo on the Base network is directly focused on accessing Coinbase's massive user base and standardized liquidity pools. Achieving deep integration into the Base network means directly touching the retail user's wallet. Robinhood Chain, however, highlights Arbitrum's technological flexibility, such as the possibility of writing smart contracts with Rust or C++ via Stylus, offering a different technical playground.

Community and Culture: Base has created a social culture with on-chain consumer dApps and events like “Onchain Summer”. Robinhood Chain's culture currently revolves more around institutional adoption, fast transactions, and DeFi efficiency.

3. What Does the Arbitrum Orbit Infrastructure Offer to NFT Projects?

Arbitrum Orbit is a permissionless framework for launching an L2 (or L3) chain. It provides crucial advantages for NFT projects that standard networks cannot offer:

Custom Gas Token: The Orbit infrastructure allows transaction fees (gas fees) to be paid with a token other than ETH. An NFT project can add massive “utility” to its ecosystem by making its own launched token the gas token of the network.

Near-Zero Transaction Fees with AnyTrust: If the network uses the Arbitrum AnyTrust data availability layer, transaction fees drop to fractions of a cent compared to the Ethereum mainnet or standard Rollups. This prevents users from entering high gas wars, especially in scenarios where thousands of NFTs are minted simultaneously.

Dedicated Throughput: On general-purpose networks like Ethereum or the main Arbitrum One network, when a popular DeFi project or another NFT mint occurs, the entire network can slow down. On a network built on Orbit or a specific Orbit chain, the capacity belongs to you and the other projects on that network, preventing performance fluctuations. This ensures an uninterrupted and fast minting experience.
